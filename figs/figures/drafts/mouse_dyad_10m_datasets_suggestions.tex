The Mouse-Dyad-10M corpus consists of 56 sessions, each recording two mice with
five synchronized cameras at 150 frames per second for approximately 20 minutes.
That is 10,084,734 frames per view and 50,423,670 across the five, from which the
corpus provides 20,169,468 proofread 3D poses and 100,847,340 2D poses
reprojected into the individual views. Each session pairs one male with one
female, drawn from nine animals, four male and five female, in 18 unique
pairings recorded between one and six times each. A 15-node mouse skeleton was
used throughout, comprising the nose, left and right ears, the tail-tip and tail
base (denoted TTI), three intermediate tail nodes, the head, trunk and neck, and
the left and right shoulders and haunches. The five cameras record at
$1280 \times 1024$ and sit between 58 and 76 degrees above the animals, so no
single view resolves the vertical axis, and each session carries its own
calibration in an aniposelib-compatible TOML file giving every camera's
intrinsics, radial distortion and extrinsic pose. The arena is
$230 \times 230 \times 140$~mm, about 6.9 body lengths across. Every session ships
the 3D reconstruction (\texttt{points3d.h5}), the per-view 2D keypoints
(\texttt{analysis.h5}), the SLEAP predictions each view was tracked from
(\texttt{predictions.slp}), the video and the calibration. The 3D was proofread
manually by three annotators, and its median reprojection error is 2.40~px, which
is the reference the geometric measurements in Figure~\ref{fig2} are scored
against. Of the 56 sessions, the 50 that also carry the shared, identity-stripped
detection pool and its transferred ground truth are the ones every tracking
benchmark uses; the remaining six carry 3D but no pool. Figures~\ref{fig2}B to
G,~\ref{fig3}E, G and H,~\ref{fig4},~\ref{fig6}A and~\ref{fig6}F were measured on
this corpus.

% ONE SENTENCE I WOULD ADD SOMEWHERE, BECAUSE FIGURE 4 DEPENDS ON IT
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
% The whole male/female result in Figure 4 rests on a convention the Datasets
% section never states. Track slot 0 is the male and slot 1 the female in all 56
% sessions -- I verified it: the seven identifiers that ever occupy slot 0 and
% the eight that ever occupy slot 1 do not overlap at all, and each identifier is
% <individual>_<index>, so they collapse to four male and five female
% individuals. Without this, a reader cannot tell how sex was assigned, and
% Figure 4's central claim looks like it came from an arbitrary track label.
% Methods/behavior states the convention; Datasets is where a reader will look
% for it. Suggested:

Animal identity is recorded per session as \texttt{<individual>\_<index>}, and
the assignment is fixed across the corpus, with the male always in the first
track slot and the female always in the second. No identifier appears in both
slots in any of the 56 sessions, which is what allows the analysis in
Figure~\ref{fig4} to be reported by sex rather than by track label.
